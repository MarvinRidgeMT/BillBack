## 1. Who is the intended user?

Uninsured or underinsured people with low incomes who got a hospital bill, and the family members who handle the
paperwork for them, in English or Spanish. Nonprofit hospitals must offer free or discounted care to low-income patients,
but the rules sit in long PDF policies that differ from hospital to hospital; KFF Health News found hospitals billed
$2.7 billion in one year to patients who probably qualified. BillBack is for the patient holding one of those bills.

What they need: a phone with a camera or any browser, the bill (or just its details typed in), and their household size
and yearly income. No account, no app, no printer.

## 2. What did you build during the event?

BillBack started at EvoHacks II on Nov 22, 2026 (photo of a bill → AI reads it → plain-code rules engine → result with
the hospital's own policy line → application PDF in English or Spanish). During this challenge (Nov 23–28) we made every
claim measurable and every failure handled:

- **One command, every number:** `python eval/run_all.py` runs all evals and writes `docs/RESULTS.md`.
- **AI vs. a non-AI baseline:** a Tesseract-OCR-plus-rules bill reader, scored on the same bills by photo condition.
- **Grounded policy reader:** every rule shown with its value, the exact source sentence, the page and a confidence;
  code checks the sentence is really in the PDF and sanity-checks the numbers (100–600% of the poverty line, free ≤
  discount, 30–365-day windows). Anything that fails is shown as "needs checking", never used silently.
- **Live mode for hospitals we've never seen:** paste a policy link or upload the PDF → read just for this session, in
  memory only → result → application. Tested on Mad River Community Hospital (California), which isn't in our set:
  read in 8.1 seconds with the policy's own sentence and page for each rule, then a result and an application PDF. Clear English/Spanish messages when the link isn't a PDF, the page has no policy,
  the PDF is password-protected or empty, or the download times out, plus "type the limits yourself" as a last resort.
- **Failure handling:** blurry-photo detection with "Retake" or "Enter by hand" (pre-filled with what was read),
  retries with backoff when the AI is down, input checks, all through one shared status component.
- **"My hospital isn't listed":** fuzzy hospital search, how to find the policy, and a link to Dollar For.
- **Tests:** exactly 200% of the poverty line, $1 over, households of 1, 8, 9+ and 20, Alaska and Hawaii, the official
  2026 poverty table, and 10 households end to end.

## 3. How does it use AI, in the product and during development?

**In the product, AI reads; code decides; the patient confirms.** Claude Sonnet 5.5 does exactly two extraction jobs
with a JSON schema on every call: it reads five fields off the bill photo, and it turns a hospital's policy into rules
(free-care limit, discount limits, asset test, residency rule, application window), each with a word-for-word quote,
page and confidence. It never decides eligibility: a plain-Python rules engine does, using the 2026 HHS poverty
guidelines. Anything under 0.8 confidence, any quote code can't find in the PDF, and any number that fails a sanity check
goes to the patient or is reported as "we can't tell".

**Why this model:** on our 12 test bills the AI read 57 of 60 fields, against 37 of 60 for a Tesseract-OCR-plus-rules baseline. They tie on clean bills; on the photos patients actually take the baseline drops to 27–70% (angled, crumpled, dim, blurry) while the AI stays at 80–100%. More important for patients: the baseline returned 15 wrong values (an amount off by 9 cents, a date read as "99/21/2026") with nothing to flag them, while the AI's 3 misses were all blanks it marked as unsure, on a deliberately very blurry photo our image check flagged. ## 5. What are the limits, and how does it handle failure?

- "Likely", not a guarantee; not legal or financial advice. The hospital decides, and the patient sends the application.
- Policy-reader accuracy against a key verified by a person is **pending**: nobody has finished hand-checking the 120
  fields yet, so we don't claim a number. What we do measure: 64 of 67 policy quotes were found word for word in the hospital's PDF, and code flagged 34 of 110 rule fields across 22 policies as "needs checking".
- Claude Sonnet 5.5 takes no temperature or seed, so we measured repeatability: the same bill read 5 times gave identical values for 3 of 3 bills. Marshfield's policy first gave a different eligibility result in 2 of 5 runs; we traced it to an ambiguity in our prompt (a residency rule waived for emergencies), fixed it, and after that eligibility was identical in 15 of 15 runs (one informational field still varies).
- End to end, 9 of 10 test households match the hand-worked answer. The miss, an out-of-state patient at a hospital whose policy covers only its service area, gets "we can't tell" instead of "probably not eligible": safe, but not right, and reported rather than hidden.
- One policy shape the schema can't hold (Novant: different free-care limits for insured and uninsured patients) is
  flagged for a person instead of guessed.
- Live mode is beta: it works on hospitals we've never seen, but its answers for them haven't been hand-checked yet.
- Failure handling, each tested: blurry or dark photos (image check → retake or type it in, pre-filled); AI errors,
  timeouts and rate limits (3 tries with backoff in a 30-second budget, then a plain message and the manual form;
  curated hospitals don't need the AI after the bill); links that aren't policies, password-protected or empty PDFs
  and download timeouts (a message and a path forward); hospital not listed (search, how to find the policy, Dollar
  For); invalid household answers (inline, in both languages).
- Privacy: nothing is stored. The bill image is sent to the AI provider to be read, processed in memory and discarded.
